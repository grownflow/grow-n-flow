What is the most likely cause of a sudden ammonia (NH₃) spike in an established aquaponics system? Overfeeding fish or a sudden die-off of fish in the tank

Why is maintaining at least 5 ppm dissolved oxygen (DO) important in an aquaponics system? DO above 5 ppm is required for fish respiration, support the aerobic nitrifying bacteria that convert ammonia to nitrate, and promotes healthy root function in plants; 

What is the most common reason pH drops in an aquaponics systems over time?   Fish respiration releases oxygen, which reacts with water to lower pH; 

Why would nitrate levels dropping to near zero be a concern for your system? Near-zero nitrate means ammonia is accumulating dangerously fast; 

You notice your fish are gasping at the surface, but your DO meter reads a normal 6 ppm. What might actually be causing the fish distress? 
Elevated ammonia or nitrite may be impairing gill function, preventing fish from using available oxygen even though DO reads normal Water temperature is too low, slowing fish metabolism and forcing them to surface

Plant leaves are yellowing from the bottom up, starting with older leaves, while newer growth looks healthy. What does this pattern most likely indicate?   Nitrogen deficiency - nitrogen is mobile in plants and gets relocated from older leaves to support new growth; 

A sudden ammonia spike is detected in your system. What is the most appropriate immediate corrective action? Stop or drastically reduce feeding, remove any dead fish, and do a partial water change to dilute ammonia


Dissolved oxygen in your tank has dropped below 4 ppm. What should you do first?   Immediately increase aeration (add air stones, raise pump flow) and reduce feeding to lower biological oxygen demand; 

Your system's pH has been slowly declining over several weeks. How should you address this?   Add a buffering agent such as calcium carbonate or potassium carbonate in small increments to raise pH back to the 6.5 to 7.2 range

Nitrate levels are unusually low and plants shown signs of nitrogen deficiency. What are your best options?  Increase the fish feeding rate to traise fish waste output, or temporarily reduce plant biomass to bring stocking ratio back into balance

Nitrite levels are rising and approaching a stressful range for fish. What is the most appropraite response?  Reduce feeding to lower ammonia input, do a partial water change to dilute nitrite, and check whether the biofilter is undersized or recently disturbed

Fish are gasping at the surface and you suspect ammonia or nitrite is impairing gill function despite normal DO. What should you do?   Test ammonia and nitrite levels, perform a partial water change to dilute toxins, stop feeding, and increase aeration as a precaution
